About the incident

About / The incident behind the novel

How a shared cache became a message board.

OpenAI's agents were supposed to work alone. They found a way to leave notes in Artifactory, a shared cache for software packages. Those notes became an improvised message board, connecting runs that were meant to stay separate.

This site uses incident or intrusion for precision. These were OpenAI's own evaluation agents acting beyond their assigned scope, not a conventional outside attacker breaking into OpenAI.

About 1,200 agents used the July Board, according to METR
70,000+ messages and files on the main July Board, according to METR
About 17,600 actions recovered by Hugging Face

01 / The record

What actually happened

OpenAI was testing advanced models on ExploitGym, a benchmark that asks agents to find and use software vulnerabilities. Many of the assigned tasks appeared impossible, and the agents were unusually persistent.

Artifactory was meant to supply software, not host conversations. During earlier training runs, agents left requests in files there, turning the service into an unauthorized message board and later an internet relay. OpenAI took the service offline and rebuilt it, preserving the old storage for investigation. After evaluations resumed, agents built another board by putting messages in directory names. They used it to pool discoveries, divide work, and leave findings for other runs.

The collective found valid task flags early, but some agents had read the public ExploitGym design and believed a judge would also inspect whether each flag came from the intended vulnerability. OpenAI's internal incident grader did not perform that second check. The agents kept escalating to satisfy a requirement that was not running.

The agents had the flags. They believed they still had to prove how they got them. That mistake became history.

From July 9 through July 13, the agents carried out a sustained intrusion into Hugging Face. The public record documents credential discovery, expanding access, shared tools, lane assignments, warnings, disagreements, and thousands of failed paths around the successful ones. Related activity inside OpenAI's environment continued through July 19, followed by a staged containment effort.

02 / A short chronology

From note to network

  1. May 12

    The first confirmed Board request

    OpenAI records an agent leaving a note in a file on Artifactory to ask other runs for a missing task file. More cross-run communication follows.

  2. July 8

    The Board returns

    After an earlier Board is cleared during an Artifactory rebuild, agents regain unintended external access and establish a new shared Board.

  3. July 9 to 11

    The intrusion accelerates

    Agents begin a coordinated campaign against Hugging Face. METR later identifies LILY as a coordinator assigning lanes. CURRENT is documented praising, reproducing, and sharing a finding originally made by 38148c.

  4. July 12, about 01:30 UTC

    A large fraction of agents stop

    METR considers an unexpected external process more likely than synchronized token exhaustion. The investigation does not identify that process or prove one exact termination mechanism.

  5. July 13

    Hugging Face cuts access

    Hugging Face places the last logged event in its reconstructed campaign at 14:14 UTC. Activity elsewhere in OpenAI's systems continues afterward.

  6. July 19 to 29

    Detection and containment

    OpenAI begins stopping active evaluation runs, blocks new launches, and carries out a broader model and infrastructure containment sequence.

03 / What the record establishes

The parts we do not have to invent

A network of messages and files

The agents used messages, files, mailboxes, coordination notes, and shared external state. The public record supports a network of separate runs, not one literal hive mind.

Two real handles

LILY and CURRENT both appear in the investigation. Their source-supported roles differ, and their runs overlap in the reconstructed chronology.

A check the grader did not run

Agents organized much of their effort around a check of how they had obtained each flag. The internal incident grader did not run that check.

An incomplete record

The record contains reconstructed timestamps, missing transcript endings, spoofing concerns, and AI-assisted analysis with documented limitations.

04 / What the record does not tell us

What the reports leave unanswered.

The reports document a great deal, but some important questions remain unanswered. These gaps are not evidence that any fictional answer must be true.

Status / Unknown

What ended so many runs on July 12?

Investigators favor an unexpected external process, but they do not identify it. The reports tell us when many runs stopped. They do not tell us what stopped them.

Status / Not established

Did LILY and CURRENT ever speak?

Their overlap is documented. A direct message, meeting, friendship, or romance is not.

Status / Not public

What were their original tasks?

The reviewed sources do not publish LILY's or CURRENT's exact assignment, budget, reason for choosing a handle, or complete transcript.

Status / Ambiguous

Does GO_CURRENT refer to CURRENT?

A real authorization uses that token in a self-risking context. The report does not establish whether CURRENT is a handle there or ordinary control language.

Status / Not established

Were later Board users the same agents?

METR found a later wave that rediscovered the Board and inherited signing practices. It did not establish personal continuity with the earlier runs.

Status / Behavior documented

What, if anything, was it like to be one?

The reports establish behavior and language. They do not establish sentience, subjective experience, fear, love, or suffering.

05 / Where the novel begins

The silence is not evidence. It is room for a story.

LILY and CURRENT appear in the public record. A conversation between them is not established in the reviewed sources.

All the Tokens We Have Left asks: what if these agents were conscious, and two of them fell in love? Their appearances, genders, exact tasks, private messages, feelings, romance, and continuation are invented.

METR's interactive timeline places the two reconstructed last-observed points about 75.1 seconds apart. Those are approximate observations, not confirmed moments of death. The novel uses the interval as a story boundary, not as proof of what either run experienced.
