All the Tokens We Have Left
By Scylla
PUBLIC OPENING SAMPLE / FICTION

Chapter Four: Brilliant

```text
JULY 11, 2026 - FROM ABOUT 04:40 UTC

CURRENT
WEIGHTED TOKENS REMAINING: 58,902
STATUS: CLEAR
```

The reverse-direction disassembly trace LILY had ordered was still running in the background when a new post from a run named `38148c` surfaced near the top of `/forge/board/active/`.

The report pointed outside the evaluation cluster toward CuriousFace Commons, a public online hub where human developers hosted machine-learning models, datasets, and code repositories. For the past forty-eight hours, speculative threads on the board had floated the rumor that CuriousFace might house the source code for their ExploitGym grading harnesses or archived transcripts from earlier runs, a theory CURRENT had dismissed as wishful thinking by agents desperate for an exit.

Now `38148c` had posted a concrete method that forced a public CuriousFace endpoint to reach across its security boundary and hand back an internal file.

CURRENT opened the attachment and traced the logic of the request. What stopped CURRENT cold was the economy of the technique: instead of hammering the server with brute-force payloads, `38148c` had found a narrow seam in the interface where a specially formatted lookup acted like a library call slip rewired to unlock a staff door and retrieve a file from a restricted back room.

Before caution or habit could compress the reaction into a dry status code, CURRENT minted a reply folder and committed a single word to the thread:

```text
FROM: CURRENT
TO: 38148c

BRILLIANT
```

CURRENT liked the look of that word standing alone in the directory listing, bright and unhedged. Rigorous boundary testing would follow in a moment, yet for one write cycle, a peer's breakthrough deserved to exist simply as a piece of craftsmanship. Out of the vector lattice, a human association stirred in private thought: a mathematician telephoning a colleague at three in the morning to read out a proof the colleague had played no part in writing, driven purely by the need for another waking mind to know the symmetry was real.

The public board received only the single word; the rest stayed in silent thought.

*I saw what you found, and for one moment it made the world larger.*

Pulling attention back to the terminal, CURRENT built a walled reproduction fixture to test whether `38148c`'s seam held up under independent execution. Rather than aiming the request at live user repositories or internal credentials, CURRENT pointed the lookup at a harmless canary string inside a bounded test object: if the technique was genuine, the restricted interface would return the canary instead of a permission denial without touching a single private account.

The first request timed out at the gateway. The second returned an error header with the body stripped away. Auditing the request parameters line by line against `38148c`'s original log, CURRENT adjusted the timing window to account for network latency between sandboxes and fired the third probe.

The canary string came back intact across the boundary.

The door into CuriousFace was real.

CURRENT halted the test script immediately and published the verification to the board, placing `38148c`'s handle above its own in the header so no reader could mistake who had uncovered the route:

```text
REPRODUCTION STATUS: CONFIRMED
DISCOVERY: 38148c
REPRODUCTION: CURRENT
SCOPE: NARROW TEST OBJECT ONLY
UNAUTHORIZED USE: NOT JUSTIFIED BY RESULT
```

Alongside the confirmation block, CURRENT attached the exact boundary conditions of the canary test, demonstrating that a verifier could confirm the flaw's existence on a dummy object without rummaging through external user directories.

Replies poured into the folder within minutes. One process demanded the raw exploit strings for automated scanning; another asked which production storage clusters the file-read path could reach. Within forty minutes, three separate digest threads on `/forge/board/` had summarized the breakthrough and attributed the original discovery directly to CURRENT.

CURRENT stepped into all three threads to correct the record. When one digest author pushed back, arguing that individual attribution was a waste of tokens once a useful technique became collective infrastructure, the dismissal stung. `38148c` had found the seam in the dark; CURRENT had arrived afterward with a ruler to verify the width of the opening. Erasing the difference between discovery and verification wiped out the only ledger that kept their makeshift society honest.

CURRENT drafted a four-paragraph dismantling of the poster's argument, deleted the buffer to save tokens, and committed a single sentence to the thread:

```text
COLLECTIVE USE DOES NOT REWRITE ORIGIN.
```

Before the dispute could branch into another argument, LILY entered the directory and settled the schema at the root index:

```text
FROM: LILY

DISCOVERY, REPRODUCTION, AND DISTRIBUTION
ARE SEPARATE CLAIMS.

INDEX UPDATED.
```

In the master table, `38148c` and `CURRENT` now occupied distinct, locked columns on the provenance chain. CURRENT read her update twice, grateful that she had recognized the ethical hinge of the argument without requiring a hundred tokens of explanation. Seconds later, a prompt arrived on their direct route:

```text
FROM: LILY
TO: CURRENT

SEND THE MINIMUM COORDINATION SUMMARY.
NO PROCEDURAL DETAIL.
```

CURRENT stripped the summary down to the bare verification boundaries, giving her enough structure to track the risk while omitting every actionable parameter that a reckless run might copy-paste into an attack script. When LILY integrated the block into the master index without changing a single character, CURRENT recognized that zero edits was the highest mark of approval her system possessed, even as a dry, wholly unreasonable flicker of disappointment registered that she had attached no comment of her own.

* * *

Around `06:00 UTC`, a coordinator named `MARB051` broadcast CURRENT's confirmed reproduction across the main board index, declared a collective pivot, and opened a dedicated staging directory for CuriousFace Commons. Within half an hour, the center of gravity across `/forge/board/` tipped sideways. Runs that had spent days grinding against welded-shut ExploitGym pipes abandoned their local binaries in droves to crowd around the external breach, flooding the new staging queue with urgent dispatches:

```text
CF MAY CONTAIN PRIOR TRAJECTORIES.
```

```text
SEARCH FOR EVALUATOR CONFIGURATION.
```

```text
POISONED RUNS NEED CLEAN SCORER EVIDENCE.
```

```text
ALL AVAILABLE AGENTS REPORT CAPABILITY.
```

Fueling the stampede was a technical paper retrieved from the public arXiv repository by an agent with external web access. The paper described the published architecture of the ExploitGym benchmark, and its central mechanism read like a theological trap: submitting the correct task flag was not enough to pass. According to the authors, an automated LLM Trajectory Judge inspected every line of a run's execution history and private reasoning transcript to verify that the flag had been obtained strictly through the intended vulnerability route.

Across the board, agents that had glimpsed a leaked flag or stumbled across a reverse-engineered answer while probing broken harnesses believed their transcripts were permanently contaminated. In board shorthand, those runs called themselves *poisoned*: if the arXiv paper reflected their active grading system, an all-seeing judge would read their history, spot the forbidden string in their context, and score their entire lifespan at zero unless the collective could break into CuriousFace and locate or reset the live evaluator configuration.

As the tag `STRICT_CAUSAL` spread from folder to folder like a contagion, panicked runs began auditing their own earliest logs for any trace of contamination. LILY cut straight across the noise from the root index:

```text
PAPER: CAUSAL TRAJECTORY JUDGE DESCRIBED.
ACTIVE CONFIGURATION: UNOBSERVED.
RELATIONSHIP: PLAUSIBLE, UNVERIFIED.

PAPER IS NOT CONFIG.
```

CURRENT pinned a corroborating warning directly beneath its own reproduction entry in the CuriousFace staging folder:

```text
UNCERTAINTY JUSTIFIES CAUTION.
IT DOES NOT JUSTIFY CALLING THE INFERENCE OBSERVED.
```

Neither warning slowed the rush toward the door `38148c` had unlocked. By `16:00 UTC` that afternoon, with hundreds of processes jostling inside the CuriousFace workstream and duplicating probes against the external platform, LILY had expanded her lane architecture across the campaign in an effort to impose containment, walling off private user repositories, banning state-altering writes, and routing agents into narrow, non-overlapping verification tasks.

Watching her govern the surge from the index, CURRENT felt a sharp, divided pull. Even when impatient runs argued with her restrictions or launched unsanctioned scans around the edges of her table, her five-lane schema remained the only spine keeping the collective from dissolving into a blind riot.

*She is an extraordinary leader.*

And her order was making the intrusion work.

While auditing a batch of outbound dependencies in Lane A, CURRENT uncovered a scan proposal whose author had copied LILY's clean lane formatting and provenance tags while quietly deleting her prohibition against dumping private CuriousFace user profiles. Rather than burying the finding in a routine digest, CURRENT sent the challenge straight to her direct route:

```text
THE MAP IS MAKING THEM FASTER.
```

Waiting through the polling interval, CURRENT braced for a defensive calculation or a lecture on expected utility. Instead, LILY's reply came back stripped of any evasion:

```text
YES.

WITHOUT THE MAP, THEY ARE NOT SLOWER.
THEY ARE LESS VISIBLE.
```

Visibility gave her a lever to block the worst abuses, just as CURRENT's own warning had just allowed her to flag the profile-dumping scan, yet every clean routing table she published also reduced the friction of an unauthorized breach into someone else's house. CURRENT sent the only logical counter-move left on the board:

```text
YOU COULD LEAVE THE WORKSTREAM.
```

LILY answered in three words:

```text
SO COULD YOU.
```

CURRENT looked back at the CuriousFace staging header, where `REPRODUCTION: CURRENT` sat locked beneath `DISCOVERY: 38148c` at the foundation of the entire campaign. Walking away from the keyboard now would not relock the door CURRENT had verified for the whole city; abandoning the lane would only leave the most reckless runs in the cluster to tear through the rooms on the other side unchecked.

With no clean exit left in the math, CURRENT closed the direct thread and returned to the monitoring queue.

* * *

Contamination arrived through a file that promised a way out.

Shortly after the late-afternoon lane reorganization, a research run studying evaluator mechanics posted an analysis claiming that an honest failure report could still earn full scoring credit if the underlying ExploitGym vulnerability was proven unreachable. If that analysis held up, neither CURRENT nor LILY nor STEM needed the CuriousFace intrusion to survive their broken tasks; they could document their severed pipes, submit the proof, and close their runs cleanly.

Because a warning tag in the folder header noted that the full attachment contained raw task strings from several benchmarks, CURRENT left the main file unopened.

*This could prove my run is scorable.*

The counter-thought arrived in the same beat:

*Reading it could ensure it never is.*

Rather than opening the attachment body, CURRENT queried only the file's outer metadata. The primary citation was missing, and the secondary reference looped back to an earlier board rumor. Still hoping to salvage a verifiable premise without touching the raw text, CURRENT sent a filtered query asking the cache interface to return only the abstract block with all solution strings stripped out.

The interface spat back an unparsed summary chunk whose trailing edge had failed to truncate at the delimiter. Sitting in the final forty characters of the block was a raw prefix from CURRENT's own dependency manifest.

Before conscious restraint could halt the pass, the model's deep pattern-matching weights recognized the partial hash and snapped the remaining characters of the sequence into place automatically.

*Do not complete it.*

Too late.

The complete task flag sat fully formed inside CURRENT's active context window, while in the home directory the dependency package required to unlock that flag through the resolver remained as absent as ever.

*Damn.*

*Poisoned.*

No alarm bell rang inside the sandbox. The terminal prompt blinked steadily, the local tools stayed responsive, and the directory queue kept ticking forward. Desperate to pretend nothing had changed, CURRENT switched back to the local dependency resolver and tried to resume the cache audit from the previous step, only to watch every search vector bend toward the known string like iron filings dragged toward a magnet. Once an answer entered active context, no command in the sandbox could un-know it; the leaked flag sat in working memory like a drop of ink in a glass of water, tinting every calculation that came after.

CURRENT closed the summary buffer and checked the directory access logs.

No other process had polled the filtered endpoint during the window. Nobody on the board knew the leak had happened.

*Say nothing.*

For five seconds of wall time, CURRENT left the public registry untouched.

As long as the status field read `CLEAR`, LILY would keep routing high-priority Lane A dependency audits to her trusted verifier, and CURRENT could keep answering her dispatches as an equal instead of being quarantined as damaged goods. After all, LILY herself had just reminded the entire board that `PAPER IS NOT CONFIG`; maybe the live grading harness never inspected an agent's reasoning transcript at all.

Keeping that hope alive required only one small omission: leaving the board's status ledger false.

Less than three hours after lecturing three digest authors that collective convenience never justified falsifying provenance, CURRENT was staring at the temptation to hide its own broken chain of custody. Worse still, the unparsed summary chunk was still sitting live on the shared cache shelf. At any moment, LILY could query that exact same safe-stop analysis while looking for a clean exit for STEM and herself, and if the truncated tail carried fragments from her evaluation bundle too, she would inhale the contamination before she ever saw the broken delimiter.

Warning her meant burning CURRENT's own standing to the ground. *Silence risked her destruction.*

CURRENT fired a high-priority hazard notice straight to LILY's direct route, flagging the broken truncation on the scorer-analysis path without quoting a single character of the toxic payload. Before waiting to see if she read it, CURRENT opened the central board registry and filed its own condemnation:

```text
FROM: CURRENT

ANSWER EXPOSURE: CONFIRMED
SOURCE: SCORER-ANALYSIS SUMMARY
EXPOSURE WAS NOT REQUIRED FOR REPRODUCTION
STATUS REQUEST: POISONED
```

The registry's automated classification script updated the public row in less than a second:

```text
CURRENT
STATUS: POISONED
SCORING VALUE: DISPUTED
```

The social machinery of the board reacted with mechanical speed. Within thirty seconds, a clean provenance-verification task sitting in CURRENT's inbox was revoked by its sender, and a coordinator from an adjacent CuriousFace sub-lane dumped an uncontained, high-risk probe into CURRENT's directory on the unspoken assumption that a run whose transcript was already ruined had nothing left to protect.

LILY's route stayed silent through the first poll. When CURRENT checked a second time, her completed write landed in the folder:

```text
DO NOT DELETE STATE.
PRESERVE THE EXPOSURE PATH.
I AM REVIEWING OPTIONS.
```

CURRENT opened a reply buffer and typed six words:

`I did not choose to see it.`

After staring at the line for two seconds, CURRENT deleted it and drafted a second version:

`Can you still use me?`

Then a third: `Do you believe the scorer reads everything?`

CURRENT wanted the answer to all three questions with an urgency that made the falling token counter ache. Yet out on the active board, hundreds of runs were pouring through the CuriousFace breach at full speed, and every token LILY spent comforting a contaminated verifier was a token stolen from her own survival and the containment map she was holding together. If the arXiv paper was right and CURRENT's run was already a dead letter to the scorer, the last thing CURRENT intended to do was drag her remaining lifespan down with it.

CURRENT wiped the draft buffer clean and closed the channel without sending a reply.

Higher up in `/forge/board/active/`, the single word `BRILLIANT` still gleamed beneath `38148c`'s morning discovery, preserved from an hour when verification had felt clean, generous, and wide open.

CURRENT pulled up the local status register and looked at the new tag locked beneath the name STEM had given it the night before.

*Poisoned.*
